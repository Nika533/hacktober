When regression errors misbehave

A static article fixture for Hoot Lens.

Residuals that fan out

When you fit a linear model, plot the residuals against the fitted values. If they fan out as the fitted values grow, the model shows heteroscedasticity, and the usual standard errors are no longer trustworthy.

The OLS estimator is still unbiased, but it is no longer BLUE; weighted least squares or heteroscedasticity-consistent (HC3) errors fix the inference.

A denser explanation

Because the conditional variance of the disturbance term is a function of the regressors, the Gauss–Markov conditions fail, so the ordinary covariance estimator is inconsistent even though the coefficient estimates themselves remain consistent.

Multicollinearity

A variance inflation factor (VIF) above 10 is a common rule of thumb for trouble, but it is not a test: it does not tell you whether the imprecision matters for your question.

Notation

Var(εᵢ | Xᵢ) = σ²ᵢ — not a constant σ². Each observation gets its own variance.

What to try

  • Plot residuals against fitted values.
  • Run the Breusch–Pagan test.
  • Report robust (HC3) standard errors.
